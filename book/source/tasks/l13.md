# L13: hold the heading the driver left

## What you will have when this is done

Let go of the turn stick and the robot keeps pointing where it was left. Push the turn stick and the
driver has it back at once. The nose stops wandering while the robot drives, which is what a driver
notices first.

## Before you start

- [L11](l11.md) is finished, because this drives field relative through the same call.
- About 2 m by 2 m of clear floor.

You write six lines in one file, `L13HeadingHoldOpMode.java`. The class that does the holding,
`HeadingHold`, is written for you.

## The steps

### Step 1: build the hold from the tuned controller

`bindings()` is where an OpMode sets up the things it will use. TODO 1 builds a `HeadingHold` out of
`Constants.foresightConfig.headingFeedback.get()`.

That is the same controller the autonomous steers with. Tuning the robot for autonomous therefore
tunes the teleop hold as well, and there is no second set of numbers to keep in step.

### Step 2: ask it for the turn power

TODO 2 replaces the turn stick with what `HeadingHold` says. Hand it the follower and the turn
stick, and it hands back the turn power to use. Then drive field relative with that in place of
the stick, and publish the two heading values so Panels can draw the hold.

What the class does with it, worth reading before you call it: while the stick is pushed it forgets
any target and passes the stick straight through. The loop the stick is released on becomes the
target. After that it feeds the difference to the controller, which steers back.

Its own deadband is 0.05, so a stick that is nearly centred counts as released. That is L3's idea
again, inside somebody else's class.

**You'll know it worked when** this prints `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LessonsTest.l13*'
```

**If it didn't**, open the report gradle names.

- `while steering, the stick wins expected:<-0.5> but was:<0.0>`. Both blanks are still open, so
  nothing turns at all.
- `NullPointerException: Cannot invoke ... HeadingHold.turn ... because "this.heading" is null`.
  TODO 2 is written and TODO 1 is not. The loop is asking a hold that was never built.
- `while steering, the stick wins expected:<-0.5> but was:<0.5>`. The turn stick is not negated.
  Pushing the right stick right swings the nose right, which is clockwise, which is a negative
  turn.
- `it steers back`, with no numbers. The stick went straight to `fieldRelative` and `HeadingHold`
  was never asked. Steering still works, which is why the first two checks pass, and nothing
  holds.

No test watches the two log lines.

### Step 3: drive it and then push it

Send the code to the robot. Run `L13 Heading Hold` and press PLAY. Drive a long diagonal with the
left stick and do not touch the turn stick.

**You'll know it worked when** the nose stays where it was. Then take hold of the robot and twist
it a few degrees. It should come back on its own.

**If it didn't**, and the robot fights you and overshoots, that is the controller's tuning rather
than this lesson. `task.autotune` is where those numbers come from.

**If it didn't**, and the nose slowly drifts anyway, the hold is working and the follower's idea
of the heading is drifting with it. That is the localizer, and [task.l8](l8.md) is where that
comes from.

:::{admonition} What has not been done here
:class: note
Step 3 has not been performed on a robot for this page. The messages in step 2 are measured.
:::

## What you just did

Three things now want to turn the robot, and they take turns in a fixed order. The driver's stick
beats the hold. The hold beats doing nothing. Nothing here beats the follower, because the
follower is not steering in teleop.

That order is the whole design. It is the same shape as L6's commanded wheels. The person with
their hands on the gamepad wins, and everything else fills in when they let go.
