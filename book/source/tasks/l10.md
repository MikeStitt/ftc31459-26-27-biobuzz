# L10: two legs, forward then sideways

## What you will have when this is done

The robot drives forward, then slides 24 inches to its right without turning, and holds where it
ends up. Five lines, and the first autonomous that does more than one thing.

## Before you start

- [L9](l9.md) is finished, so one leg works and the follower knows where the robot starts.
- About 1 m by 2 m of clear floor, and a tape measure.

You write five lines in one file, `L10ForwardThenStrafeOpMode.java`. Three poses are declared above
it: `start`, `corner` and `end`. Read their headings before you write anything. All three are the
same.

## The steps

### Step 1: two legs and a hold

`sequential` takes a list of commands and runs them in order. L9's list had one item. This one has
three.

TODO 1 is the first leg, `start` to `corner`. TODO 2 is the second leg, `corner` to `end`. Both hold
the heading the robot starts at, so it never turns. The second leg moves it sideways instead, which
is what mecanum wheels are for. TODO 3 is `hold(follower, end)`, which keeps the robot where it
finished.

The robot faces +y the whole way. So the first leg is straight forward, and the second one is a pure
strafe to its right.

**You'll know it worked when** this prints `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LessonsTest.l10*'
```

**If it didn't**, open the report gradle names.

- `the robot never started following a path`. The routine still hands back `Command.NOOP`.
- `never turned, so it still faces +y`. The heading changed somewhere. Pass a pose to `constant()`
  and it takes the heading from that pose, which is what you want here.
- `strafed 24 inches in x`. The first leg went straight to `end` instead of stopping at `corner`.
  The robot drives one diagonal instead of two legs, and arrives somewhere between them.

:::{admonition} `constant()` counts in radians
:class: note
`constant(90)` does not mean 90 degrees. The number form is radians, and 90 radians is about 14 full
turns; the path settles at 116.6 degrees once that wraps around. Measured on Pedro 3.0.1.

The poses use `POSES.of(x, y, degrees)` because a pose factory converts for you. `constant()` does
not. So pass it a pose, `constant(start)`, or convert first with `Math.toRadians(90)`.
:::

### Step 2: drive it on the floor

Put the robot down with tape at its front edge. Send the code. Choose `L10 Forward Then Strafe` and
press PLAY.

**You'll know it worked when** the robot drives straight forward, stops, then slides sideways to its
right and stops again. Its nose points the same way the whole time. It should end up about 24 inches
to the right of the line it drove up.

**If it didn't**, and it drove both legs but the corner was a sharp stop and restart rather than a
curve, that is what two separate legs do. The follower finishes the first line before it starts the
second.

**If it didn't**, and it slid left instead of right, check which way +x runs from where the robot is
standing. Facing +y, the robot's right hand points at +x.

:::{admonition} What has not been done here
:class: note
Step 2 has not been performed on a robot for this page. `hold` is not watched by anything either:
the test passes with TODO 3 left out. What it does on a floor is keep the wheels working against a
push once the path is over.
:::

## What you just did

An autonomous routine is a list. That is all it is. Two legs and a hold here, and a real match
autonomous is the same list with more items and a few of them moving something other than the
wheels.

A leg carries a heading, held from one end of it to the other. Hold the same heading on both legs
and the robot never turns; where it goes and which way it faces are two separate things. That is
what mecanum wheels buy, and [L11](l11.md) spends it.
