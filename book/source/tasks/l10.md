# L10: two legs, and a turn on the way

## What you will have when this is done

The robot drives 24 inches forward, then 24 inches to its left while turning a quarter turn, and
holds where it ends up. Five lines, and the first autonomous that does more than one thing.

## Before you start

- [L9](l9.md) is finished, so one leg works and the follower knows where the robot starts.
- About 1 m by 1 m of clear floor, and a tape measure.

You write five lines in one file, `L10PathWithTurnOpMode.java`. Three poses are declared above it:
`start`, `corner` and `end`. Read their headings before you write anything.

## The steps

### Step 1: two legs and a hold

`sequential` takes a list of commands and runs them in order. L9's list had one item. This one has
three.

TODO 1 is the first leg, `start` to `corner`, holding heading 0. TODO 2 is the second leg, `corner`
to `end`, holding a quarter turn. Holding a new heading on a leg is what makes the robot turn as it
drives it, which is the whole point of the lesson. TODO 3 is `hold(follower, end)`, which keeps the
robot where it finished.

Headings in the code are in radians. The poses above use `POSES.of(x, y, degrees)` because a pose
factory converts for you, but `constant()` does not, so `Math.toRadians(90)` is the call.

**You'll know it worked when** this prints `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LessonsTest.l10*'
```

**If it didn't**, open the report gradle names.

- `the robot never started following a path`. The routine still hands back `Command.NOOP`.
- `finishes facing +y expected:<90.0> but was:<113.63022487707525>`. `constant(90)` asks for 90
  radians, which is about 14 turns. The robot ends up wherever that lands after the wrapping.
- `finishes facing +y expected:<90.0> but was:<6.293731601104298>`. The first leg goes straight to
  `end` instead of stopping at `corner`. The robot drives the diagonal holding heading 0 and never
  turns, so it arrives in the right place facing the wrong way.
- `expected:<96.0> but was:<98.13850557580267>`. The second leg uses `.linear(0,
  Math.toRadians(90))`. The note below is about that one.

:::{admonition} This is where `.linear()` shows its bug
:class: note
Pedro 3.0.1 interpolates heading backwards on a line path with `.linear()`, reported in its issues
#176 and #181. L9 could not show it, because that path holds 0 at both ends and there is nothing to
interpolate. This leg turns, so it can.

Watched, with the heading read every 100 loops. Written the lesson's way it climbs 0, 46.7, 84.3,
89.4 degrees and settles. Written with `.linear(0, Math.toRadians(90))` it climbs to 46.4 degrees,
falls back to 22.9, then comes up to 90 at the end, and the robot bulges out to x = 97.8 in while it
sorts itself out instead of holding 96 in. The test stops sooner than the probe did and sees
98.14 in.

Both versions end up in the right place if you give them long enough. The path between the two ends
is what differs, and on a field that path is where the other robots are.
:::

### Step 2: drive it on the floor

Put the robot down with tape at its front edge. Send the code. Choose `L10 Path With Turn` and press
PLAY.

**You'll know it worked when** the robot drives forward, then curves left while swinging its nose
left, and stops facing a quarter turn from where it started. It should be about 24 inches forward
and 24 inches to the left of the tape.

**If it didn't**, and it drove both legs but the corner was a sharp stop and restart rather than a
curve, that is what two separate legs do. The follower finishes the first line before it starts the
second.

**If it didn't**, and the nose swung the wrong way round, check the second leg's heading. A quarter
turn counter-clockwise is `Math.toRadians(90)`, and clockwise would be the negative of it.

:::{admonition} What has not been done here
:class: note
Step 2 has not been performed on a robot for this page. Everything quoted above is from the
simulator. `hold` is not watched by anything either: the test passes with TODO 3 left out. What it
does on a floor is keep the wheels working against a push once the path is over.
:::

## What you just did

An autonomous routine is a list. That is all it is. Two legs and a hold here, and a real match
autonomous is the same list with more items and a few of them moving something other than the
wheels.

Each leg carries its own heading, held from one end of it to the other. The robot does not get a
turn instruction. It gets a leg with a new heading on it, and the follower swings the nose round
while it drives the line.
