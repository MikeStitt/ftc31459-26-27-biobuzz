Glossary¶

Every word here is one the task pages use, and each entry names the page that introduces it. Where the guide could have invented a friendlier word and did not, the reason is that the code, Pedro’s documentation and the Driver Station all use this one.

OpMode

One program the Driver Station can run. Each lesson is an OpMode, and its name in the list is the one in its @TeleOp or @Autonomous line. L2

TeleOp and Autonomous

The two kinds of OpMode. A TeleOp reads the gamepads; an autonomous runs on its own. L9 and L10 are the two autonomous lessons. L9

Driver Station

The phone or tablet that holds the gamepads and picks the OpMode. Lines printed with Tracker.printToDs show up there. L2

Panels

The web page that draws what the code published while it ran. Every Tracker.publish key shows up there, and the cheat sheet lists them. L3

Drivetrain

The four motors and the code that decides what power each one gets. Every lesson shares one class, LessonsDriveTrain, and adds to it. L2

Deadband

A band of stick readings near the middle that count as let go. Without one the robot creeps when nobody is touching it. L3

Squaring a stick

Making a small push mean a smaller power, by multiplying the stick by itself and putting the sign back. L3

Normalizing

Scaling four wheel powers down together when the biggest one is over 1. The robot still goes where it was asked. It only goes slower. L4

Mixing

Turning forward, sideways and turn into four wheel powers with four sums. L5

Mecanum

A wheel with rollers set at 45 degrees on its rim, which lets the robot drive sideways. The four wheels’ rollers make an X seen from above. L5

Commanded wheels

Four powers a lesson has set itself. The path follower cannot overrule them. It gets the wheels back when the lesson hands them back. L6

Follower

Pedro’s code that drives the robot along a path, or holds it at a pose. From L6 on it holds the drivetrain and the lessons reach past it. L6

Pose

Where the robot is and which way it faces: x, y and a heading. A pose is a place on the field, not a distance from the robot. L8

Heading

Which way the robot is facing, as an angle. Counter-clockwise counts up, and the code keeps it in radians even where a page says degrees. L8

Localizer

Whatever works out the pose. A robot can run more than one at a time. That is what L8 is for. L8

Shadow localizer

A second localizer that is read and logged but never steers anything. It is how two answers get compared on one run. L8

Dead reckoning

Working out where you are by adding up every step you took, with nothing to check it against. The drive encoders do it; the Pinpoint does it better. L8

Pinpoint

The odometry computer. It has its own two measuring wheels, and it works out the pose. The follower believes this one. L17 measures against it. L8

Path

A line or curve the follower drives along, with a heading to hold while it does. L9

Hold

Telling the follower to sit at one pose and stay there. If something pushes the robot, the follower puts it back. L10

Field relative

Sticks that mean the field. Push away from you and the robot moves away from you, whichever way its nose points. L11

Robot relative

Sticks that mean the robot’s own directions. Better for lining up against something. L12

Heading hold

Keeping the nose where the driver left it, and giving it straight back the moment the turn stick moves. L13

Instant command

A command that does its work and finishes in the same breath, rather than running for a while. A button press is usually one. L14

Feedforward

A guess at the power a wanted speed needs, made before anything is measured. Gets most of the way there at once. L16

Feedback

A correction worked out from two speeds: the one asked for and the one measured. It cleans up what the guess got wrong. L16

Ticks

What an encoder counts as a motor turns. A tick is a fixed fraction of a turn, so ticks become inches once you know how many make one. L17

Ticks per inch

How many ticks a wheel counts for an inch of travel. Measured, not looked up. L17

Turn radius

How far a wheel sits from the middle of the robot, along the diagonal it pushes. It turns radians per second into inches per second at the wheel. L17

Coast and brake

What a motor does at zero power. Braking holds the robot still; coasting lets the wheels roll, which is what a measurement you push by hand needs. L17
