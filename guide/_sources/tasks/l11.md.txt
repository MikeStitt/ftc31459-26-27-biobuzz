# L11: make the sticks mean the field

## What you will have when this is done

Push the stick away from you and the robot goes away from you. It does that whichever way the robot
happens to be facing, so a driver never has to think about which end of the robot is the front.

## Before you start

- [L6](l6.md) is finished, because this uses the same three lines it ended on.
- [L5](l5.md) is worth running again first. Turn the robot to face you, push the left stick away
  from you, and watch it drive at you. That is the problem this lesson fixes.
- About 2 m by 2 m of clear floor.

You write in two files. `LessonsDriveTrain.java` gets the rotation, TODO 9, and
`L11FieldRelativeOpMode.java` calls it.

## The steps

### Step 1: rotate the driver's directions into the robot's

The driver's two numbers are about the field. The wheels only understand the robot: forward along
its nose, and sideways across it. Turning one into the other is a rotation, and the angle is the
robot's heading.

Write `fieldRelative` in `LessonsDriveTrain.java`, TODO 9. Take the cosine and the sine of the
heading into their own variables. Then rotate the field pair **backwards** through the heading,
which is what the two lines in the TODO do. Then mix, scale and command the wheels, which is the
same three lines `sticks` ends on.

Backwards is the part worth a second look. The heading says how far the robot has turned away
from the field. To ask "what does the field's +x look like from where the robot is standing?",
you have to undo that turn.

### Step 2: hand it the heading

The drivetrain cannot work out the heading for itself. It talks to motors; it has no idea where the
robot is. The follower does, so the OpMode reads `follower.pose().heading()` and passes it in first.

Then the same three sticks as L5, negated the same way.

**You'll know it worked when** this prints `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LessonsTest.l11*'
```

**If it didn't**, open the report gradle names.

- `expected:<1.0> but was:<0.0>`, on the very first check. Nothing reached the wheels, so the
  OpMode's blank is still open.
- `same stick, still moves away from the driver expected:<0.0> but was:<1.0>`. The OpMode is still
  calling `sticks`, which is L5's door and knows nothing about the field.
- `expected:<-1.0> but was:<1.0>`. The rotation runs the wrong way. Either the two signs in
  `fieldRelative` are the other way round, or the heading was negated on the way in. Both undo the
  same thing twice.
- `same stick, still moves away from the driver expected:<0.0> but was:<-0.3338674754196965>`. The
  heading went in as degrees. `Math.cos` wants radians, and 90 radians is about 14 turns, so the
  answer is nonsense of exactly this shape.

### Step 3: drive it facing the wrong way

Send the code to the robot. Run `L11 Field Relative` and press PLAY. Turn the robot to face you, by
pushing the right stick until its nose points at you. Then push the left stick away from you.

**You'll know it worked when** the robot drives away from you while facing you. Turn it a quarter
turn and try again, and the stick still means the same direction on the field.

**If it didn't**, and the robot drives away from you at first but the directions slide as you drive
around, the follower's pose is drifting. That is the localizer, not this lesson.
[task.l8](l8.md) is where the drift comes from.

## What you just did

Field relative is one rotation, and nothing else. There is no new way of driving here: the same
`mix`, the same `normalized`, the same commanded wheels. All that changed is which pair of numbers
gets handed to them.

It also depends on the follower knowing where the robot is pointing. A localizer that has drifted 20
degrees gives a driver a robot whose sticks are 20 degrees crooked. This is the first lesson where
somebody else's mistake shows up in your hands.
