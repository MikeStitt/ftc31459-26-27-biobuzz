# L3: make the sticks feel right

## What you will have when this is done

The robot sits still when both sticks are let go, and a gently pushed stick makes it move gently.
Same wheels, same tank drive as L2. What changes is what happens to the stick number on its way to
the motors.

## Before you start

- [L2](l2.md) is finished, so `L2b Tank` drives.
- [task.directions](directions.md) is done, so a wheel that goes the wrong way is not in the way of
  reading what these two lessons do.
- About 2 m of clear floor.

You write in three files. `LessonsDriveTrain.java` gets the two new methods, and
`L3aDeadbandOpMode.java` and `L3bSquaredOpMode.java` each use one more of them than the last.

## The steps

### Step 1: ignore a stick that was let go

A stick that has been let go does not read 0. It reads a few hundredths, and a few hundredths of
power is enough to make the robot crawl across the floor on its own. A **deadband** is the answer:
any reading smaller than the band counts as let go.

Write `deadband` in `LessonsDriveTrain.java`, TODO 7. It takes the stick and the band, and answers
either 0 or the stick itself.

`Math.abs` is the part worth thinking about. A stick pulled back reads -0.5, and -0.5 is smaller
than 0.05 the way a thermometer means smaller. What you want to compare is how far from the middle
it is, with the minus sign taken off, and that is what `Math.abs` does.

Then open `L3aDeadbandOpMode.java`. TODO 1 is the band itself, and the number is your choice: 0.05
is a good first guess. TODO 2 puts each stick through `deadband` and hands the two on.

**You'll know it worked when** this prints `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LessonsTest.l3a*'
```

**If it didn't**, open the report gradle names.

- `outside the band, the stick is passed through untouched expected:<0.5> but was:<0.0>`. Nothing
  reached the wheels, so TODO 2 has not handed the two shaped sticks to `drivetrain.sticks()`.
- `inside the deadband, so the robot does not creep expected:<0.0> but was:<0.029999999329447746>`.
  The band is still 0, so nothing is ever inside it. That long number is the stick the test pushed:
  a gamepad axis is a `float`, which keeps fewer digits than a `double`, and 0.03 is not one of the
  numbers it can hold exactly.

### Step 2: drive it with your hands off the sticks

Send the code to the robot. Run `L3a Deadband`, press PLAY, and let go of both sticks.

**You'll know it worked when** the robot sits still. Then push a stick a long way and let go again,
and it stops.

**If it didn't**, and it still creeps, the band is smaller than what this gamepad reads when it is
let go. Raise it a little: 0.05 to 0.1 is the usual range. A band much bigger than that starts to
throw away real driving.

### Step 3: make a gentle push mean gently

With the deadband in, the stick is either ignored or passed straight through. Passed straight
through means a tenth of a push is a tenth of the power, and near the middle that is more robot than
a driver wants. Squaring fixes it: half a push becomes a quarter of the power, and a full push is
still full power.

Write `squared` in `LessonsDriveTrain.java`, TODO 8. Squaring a negative number the ordinary way
loses the minus sign, and a driver asking for backwards and getting forwards is worse than no
squaring at all. So work out the size first, then put the sign back.

Then fill `L3bSquaredOpMode.java`, TODO 1 and TODO 2. It deadbands each stick and then squares it,
one step to a line. Each lesson keeps its own band, so the number you picked in step 1 gets picked
again here.

**You'll know it worked when** this prints `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LessonsTest.l3b*'
```

**If it didn't**, open the report.

- `and squaring keeps the sign expected:<-0.25> but was:<0.25>`. The sign was not put back, so
  backwards came out forwards.
- `and squaring keeps the sign expected:<-0.25> but was:<0.0>`. This one is `deadband`, not
  `squared`: `Math.abs` is missing, so every negative stick reads as smaller than the band and gets
  thrown away. The robot drives forwards and refuses to back up. The L3a test does not catch it,
  because every stick it pushes outside the band is a forwards one.

### Step 4: drive it gently

Run `L3b Squared`. Push a stick about a third of the way and hold it.

**You'll know it worked when** the robot creeps along under control. Push the stick all the way and
it still gives everything the robot has.

**If it didn't**, and it backs up but will not go forward, the sign is wrong. Or the other way
round. Step 3's second message is the same fault, seen from the bench.

## What you just did

Both new methods sit between the stick and the wheels. Neither one knows what a wheel is. That is
why they live in `LessonsDriveTrain` and not in the lesson. L15 and L16 drive through the same two
methods, and they call the ones you wrote here. There is no second copy. Squaring is also the first
time this guide bends what a stick means. Half a push really is a quarter of the power. So the
numbers in Panels are no longer the numbers your hand is making, and that is why
`stick/left_raw` sits beside `stick/left_shaped`. You can watch both at once.
