L4: one stick to drive, one to turn¶

What you will have when this is done¶

Arcade drive. One stick decides how fast the robot goes. The other decides how hard it turns. The robot takes the curve you asked for, even when you ask for more power than it has.

Before you start¶

  • L3 is finished, so L3b Squared drives.

  • Clear floor about 3 m across. This one drives in circles.

You write in three files: L4ArcadeDriveTrain.java, L4ArcadeOpMode.java, and one more method in LessonsDriveTrain.java.

The steps¶

Step 1: mix the two sticks into two sides¶

Tank drive asked the driver to think in left wheels and right wheels. Arcade asks for forward and turn instead, which is how a driver already thinks, and the robot works out the two sides.

Write sticks in L4ArcadeDriveTrain.java. It takes a forward speed and a turn speed, and turns them into a left speed and a right speed. Turning counter-clockwise means the left side goes slower and the right side goes faster, so one of the two gets the turn taken off it and the other gets it added on. Then hand the two to driveWheelsNow, the way L2TankDriveTrain does.

Then fill the TODO in L4ArcadeOpMode.java. Forward comes from the left stick’s y axis and turn from the right stick’s x axis. Both get a minus sign on the way in, and both get logged.

You’ll know it worked when this prints BUILD SUCCESSFUL:

./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LessonsTest.l4*'

If it didn’t, open the report gradle names.

  • turning right speeds the left wheels up expected:<0.75> but was:<0.0>. Nothing reached the wheels, so driveWheelsNow is not being called with the two speeds.

  • turning right speeds the left wheels up expected:<0.75> but was:<0.25>. The turn is going on the wrong side. Swap which one gets it added and which gets it taken off.

Step 2: share out the power when there is not enough¶

Push the drive stick all the way forward and the turn stick half over, and arcade asks one side for 1.5 . A motor cannot do 1.5, and the largest it will take is 1. Something has to give.

The wrong answer is to chop it off at 1, because that changes the shape of what the driver asked for: the forward part keeps all of what it wanted and the turn part loses a third of what it wanted, so the robot drives a wider curve than the sticks describe. The right answer is to divide every wheel by the biggest of them, which keeps the shape and makes the whole thing slower.

Write normalized in LessonsDriveTrain.java, TODO 3. Find the biggest of the four with the minus signs taken off, or 1 if none of them reaches 1, and divide every power by it. Start the biggest at 1, not at 0: starting at 0 would scale every gentle request up to full power.

You’ll know it worked when step 3 says so on the floor. No test catches this one. The L4 test passes with normalized not written at all. The motors already cut anything over 1 down to 1, and the two stick values that test uses come out the same either way.

If it didn’t, the test that passed in step 1 may now fail. Look for turning right speeds the left wheels up expected:<0.75> but was:<1.0>. That one means the biggest starts at 0 rather than 1. A half-speed request is being scaled up to full.

Step 3: drive two circles¶

Run L4 Arcade. Hold the drive stick half forward and the turn stick a quarter over, and let the robot go all the way round. Then drive the same circle with both sticks twice as far over: drive all the way, turn half.

You’ll know it worked when the two circles are the same size. The second one is faster, and that is the only difference.

If it didn’t, and the second circle is much wider than the first, normalized is not dividing. In the simulator the first circle has a radius of 32.2 in either way. The second is 32.2 in with normalized written and 48.3 in without it, which is half again as wide.

Where those two radii come from

The simulator, not a floor. L4 Arcade was run for 1 s at each pair of sticks, with normalized written and then deleted, and the radius worked out from where the robot ended up. A real floor has grip and slip in it, so read the shape rather than the number.

What you just did¶

normalized is the first code here that does not decide where the robot goes. It decides what to do when the driver asks for more than the robot has. Its answer is the same thing, slower. That is why it divides instead of cutting off. It also writes down what it divided by, as powerScale. A log can then say how much of what the driver asked for really happened. Every lesson from here on gets this for free, because driveWheelsNow already calls it.
