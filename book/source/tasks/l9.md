# L9: the first autonomous

## What you will have when this is done

The robot drives 24 inches forward on its own and stops there. Nobody touches a stick. This is the
first lesson where the follower does the driving, and it is three lines long.

## Before you start

- [L6](l6.md) is finished. The follower has to be holding a real drivetrain to drive anything.
- About 1 m of clear floor in front of the robot, and a tape measure.
- [L8](l8.md) comes before this one in the guide, and nothing here needs it. The follower uses the
  robot's own localizer, not yours.

You write three lines in one file, `L9Drive24OpMode.java`.

## The steps

### Step 1: say where to go

An autonomous has no `loop` full of sticks. It has a `routine`, and the scheduler runs it. Your job
is to hand back a command that says what to do.

The command is a sequence of one step: follow a straight line from `start` to `end`, holding the
heading the robot starts at. Both poses are already declared above, 24 inches apart along y. The
robot faces +y, so that straight line is straight forward.

`startPose()` is already written, and it matters. The follower has no idea where the robot is until
something tells it, and everything after that is measured from there.

**You'll know it worked when** this prints `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LessonsTest.l9*'
```

**If it didn't**, open the report gradle names.

- `the robot never started following a path`. The routine still hands back `Command.NOOP`, which is
  the command that does nothing.
- `ends 24 inches further along y`. The line runs from `end` to `start`. The robot is standing at
  that path's finish already, so the command is done the moment it starts and nothing moves.
- `and still faces +y`. `constant(90)` does not mean 90 degrees. The number form is radians. Pass a
  pose instead, `constant(start)`, and it takes the heading from that pose.

:::{admonition} Why `.constant()` and not `.linear()`
:class: note
`.constant()` holds one heading for the whole line, which is what this path wants: the heading is
the same at both ends, so there is nothing to interpolate.

`.linear()` interpolates between two headings, and on Pedro 3.0.1 it does it backwards on a line
path, reported in its issues #176 and #181. Nothing in this lesson can show that, because both ends
hold the same heading.
:::

### Step 2: drive it on the floor

Put the robot on the floor with a piece of tape at its front edge. Send the code. Choose `L9 Drive
24` from the autonomous list, and press PLAY.

**You'll know it worked when** the robot drives forward, slows down, and stops. Measure from the
tape to the front edge: it should be close to 24 inches.

**If it didn't**, and it overshot or stopped short, that is the follower's tuning, not your three
lines. `task.autotune` is where the numbers behind it come from.

**If it didn't**, and it curved instead of going straight, check
[task.directions](directions.md) first. A backwards wheel curves every drive, with the sticks or
without them.

The driver station shows the pose, the speed and the loop rate while it runs, because `loop` asks it
to. Watch the y number climb by 24 while x stays put.

:::{admonition} What has not been done here
:class: note
Step 2 has not been performed on a robot for this page. The 24 inches and the two poses are read
from the lesson and from its test, which drives the same path in the simulator.
:::

## What you just did

The follower is a library, and this lesson uses it the way a team uses one: you say where to go and
it works out the powers. You did not write the path algorithm and you do not need to be able to.
What you did write is everything the follower needs to know about this robot, back in L6.

`sequential` takes a list, and this one has a single item in it. L10 puts a second item after it,
and that is the whole of what an autonomous routine is: a list of things to do in order.
