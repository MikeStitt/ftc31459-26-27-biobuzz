L5: slide it sideways¶

What you will have when this is done¶

The robot slides sideways without turning. The left stick drives it and slides it, the right stick turns it, and all three can happen at once. Everything is relative to the robot’s own nose, whichever way the nose happens to be pointing.

Before you start¶

  • L4 is finished, so normalized is written. L5 needs it. Asking for forward and sideways in the same push asks for more than full power, and normalized is what sorts that out.

  • task.directions is done.

  • Mecanum wheels on all four corners, with their rollers making an X seen from above. Step 2 says how to read that.

  • About 2 m by 2 m of clear floor.

You write in two files, L5HolonomicDriveTrain.java and L5HolonomicOpMode.java, and the test needs both.

fig-mecanum-x

The robot from above with its nose marked, and the top roller of each of the four wheels drawn as a line at 45 degrees, so the four lines make an X across the robot.

The steps¶

Step 1: mix all four wheels¶

A mecanum wheel has rollers set at 45 degrees. Spinning it pushes the robot along and sideways at the same time. Spin all four forward and the sideways pushes cancel, so the robot drives forward. Spin one diagonal forward and the other diagonal back and the forward pushes cancel instead. What is left is a slide.

So there are three numbers now, not two: forward, slide left, turn. Each corner adds the three up its own way.

Open L5HolonomicDriveTrain.java. The TODO asks for four named powers and one call to driveWheelsNow. The four lines are written out in the class javadoc just above, and so are three places that draw where the signs come from.

Then open L5HolonomicOpMode.java. The left stick’s x axis is the new one, and it needs negating like the other two. The stick reads positive to the right, and strafeLeftSpeed counts positive to the left.

You’ll know it worked when this prints BUILD SUCCESSFUL:

./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LessonsTest.l5*'

If it didn’t, open the report gradle names.

  • driving forward turns every wheel the same way expected:<1.0> but was:<0.0>. Nothing reached the wheels at all, so one of the two files is still blank.

  • sliding left runs one diagonal back and the other forward expected:<-1.0> but was:<1.0>. The slide goes the wrong way. Either the minus sign is missing on gamepad1.left_stick_x, or the strafe signs are swapped in the four lines. The two mistakes look the same from here.

  • turning right runs the left side forward expected:<0.5> but was:<-0.5>. Same fault, on the turn instead of the slide.

  • expected:<1.0> but was:<-1.0>, with no words in front of it. Only the first check of each group carries words, so this one is the back left wheel while sliding left. The two back lines have the two front lines’ strafe signs. Copying the first two lines down and editing only the turn does exactly this.

Step 2: slide it sideways on the floor¶

Send the code to the robot. Run L5 Holonomic and press PLAY. Push the left stick straight left and hold it there.

You’ll know it worked when the robot slides to its own left with the nose still pointing the same way. Push straight right and it comes back.

If it didn’t, and it slid the wrong way, that is step 1’s second message seen from the floor. The test catches it, so run the test again.

If it didn’t, and it turned instead of sliding, or ground along hardly moving, a wheel is on the wrong corner. Look down on the robot. The top roller of each wheel runs at 45 degrees, and the four of them make an X. Forward still works with a wheel in the wrong place, because all four still push forward. It is the sideways halves that stop cancelling.

Where the X comes from

L5HolonomicDriveTrain’s own javadoc and Game Manual 0’s mecanum page, which it links. No robot here has been driven with a wheel on the wrong corner, so the symptom above is what the arithmetic says, not something that has been watched.

Step 3: drive a diagonal¶

Push the left stick into a corner, forward and left together, and hold it.

You’ll know it worked when the robot goes diagonally with the nose still forward. It covers less ground each way than it would going straight, which is the point of step 3.

If it didn’t, and it drifted into a slow spin, that is the right stick at rest. L5 does not deadband anything. L3’s two methods belong to L3’s two lessons, and this one reads the sticks raw, so a stick that does not quite read 0 turns the robot.

Where these numbers come from

The simulator, held at one stick position for 1 s each time. Full forward covered 54.75 in. Full left covered 37.07 in. The corner of the stick covered 27.38 in forward and 18.53 in left, both halves of the full numbers, because normalized divided all four powers by 2.

Half into the corner measured the same as all the way into the corner. At half, the front right wheel asks for exactly 1. At full it asks for 2, and normalized divides by 2 and gives 1 again. The corner of a square stick is already full power halfway out.

A real floor is slower than this and scrubs the rollers sideways. Read the ratios rather than the numbers.

What you just did¶

Four wheels, three numbers, one line each. Those four lines are the whole of mecanum. The path follower works with the same four lines later. So a robot that slides correctly here follows a path correctly in L6.

Sliding is slower than driving. The simulator keeps two different top speeds, one along the robot and one across it, and a second of driving beat a second of sliding by 54.75 in to 37.07 in. Expect that on a floor too, and plan a route that needs less sideways than forwards.

This is also the last lesson where the drivetrain owns the wheels by itself. L5HolonomicDriveTrain has no mix, so the path follower cannot drive it: hand one to the follower and it stops at the first update and says so. That refusal is on purpose. It is what lets L2 to L5 exist without any of the follower’s arithmetic, and L6 is where the missing method arrives.

Where next¶

  • task.panels puts the numbers a lesson sends on a screen while somebody drives. From here on the lessons are easier to debug when you can watch them.

  • task.l6 hands the wheels to the path follower, and hands them back when the driver wants them.
