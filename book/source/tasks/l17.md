# L17: measure the two numbers L16 trusted

## What you will have when this is done

Two numbers in `Constants.java` that came off your robot instead of somebody else's: how many
encoder ticks make an inch, and how far a wheel sits from the middle. You get them by pushing and
spinning the robot by hand while it watches itself.

## Before you start

- [L16](l16.md) is finished. It is the lesson that uses both numbers.
- The Pinpoint is mounted and tuned, because this measurement believes it.
  [task.pinpoint](pinpoint.md) is where the mounting comes from.
- About 1.5 m of clear straight floor for the push, and room to spin the robot on the spot.

You write four lines, all in `LessonsDriveTrain.java`. Both OpModes are given.

## The steps

### Step 1: open a door to the encoders

TODO 11 writes `wheelTicks()`. Four lines, each one putting what a motor's encoder has counted into
that wheel's slot: `ticks[FL] = hardware.frontLeft.getCurrentPosition();` and three more.

Nothing here is new. It is the same shape as `writeWheels()` from L2b, pointing the other way: that
one sends four numbers out to the motors, this one brings four numbers back.

**You'll know it worked when** this prints `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`:

```
./gradlew :TeamCode:testDebugUnitTest --tests '*LessonsDriveTrainTest.theTicksDoor*'
```

**If it didn't**, the test puts 10, 20, 30 and 40 on the four encoders. So the numbers in the
message name the wheel they came from.

- `front left, front right, back left, back right: arrays first differed at element [0];
  expected:<10> but was:<0>`. The blank is still open, so every measurement would say the robot
  never moved.
- `expected:<10> but was:<20>`. Two motors went into each other's slots. 20 is the front right,
  and it is in the front left's place.
- `expected:<10> but was:<0>`, with the door written. `getVelocity()` reads how fast a wheel is
  turning, and a wheel nobody is pushing is turning at 0. `getCurrentPosition()` is the one that
  counts.

### Step 2: push the robot and measure an inch

Send the code to the robot. Run `L17a Measure ticks per inch` and press PLAY. Push the robot slowly
straight forward, at least 36 in, keeping it pointing the same way.

The robot is keeping two accounts of the same journey. The Pinpoint says how far it went in inches,
because its own wheels measure the floor. The drive encoders say how many ticks they counted. One
divided by the other is the number, and neither account knows what the other said.

Push slowly. A wheel that slips travels without turning. The encoders then count less than the
floor did, and the answer comes out short. Do it three times. Three answers a tick or two apart are
a measurement. Three that disagree mean something slipped.

**You'll know it worked when** the Driver Station shows `ticksPerInch` with a number after it. Put
that number in `Constants.ticksPerInch`, which is 45.0 until you do, and run it again to check.

**If it didn't**, and it says `Encoders counted 0 ticks` while the inches climb, step 1 is not
finished on the robot you just sent to.

**If it didn't**, and the wheels drag as you push, they are braking when they should coast. The
OpMode asks for that itself, and the two tests below say whether it works.

### Step 3: spin the robot and measure the radius

Run `L17b Measure turn radius` and spin the robot on the spot, at least two full turns, keeping
it in roughly the same place. **Run L17a first**: this one turns ticks into inches with the
number you just measured, so a wrong one there is a wrong one here.

A wheel `r` inches from the middle travels `r` inches for every radian the robot turns. So the
radius is wheel inches divided by radians, which is the same trick as step 2 with a different
pair of accounts.

**You'll know it worked when** the Driver Station shows a turn radius, and a tape measure agrees
with it. Measure the diagonal between two opposite wheels and halve it. That is the same
distance.

**If it didn't**, and the number is about half what the tape says, check that you spun the robot
rather than swinging it round the room. The Pinpoint counts the turning, and driving in a circle
adds turning the wheels never made.

:::{admonition} The wheels have to roll, and two tests say so
:class: note
Both OpModes force the wheels to coast in `start()` and put braking back in `stop()`. It is given
code, and it has been wrong twice, so both mistakes are watched.

- Put the coast back inside the loop instead of at the end and the coast lasts one loop:
  `L17aMeasureTicksPerInchOpMode still coasts five loops in`. That is how the lesson used to be
  written, and the wheels braked while the student was still pushing.
- Leave braking un-restored at the end and two messages appear:
  `L17aMeasureTicksPerInchOpMode brakes again afterwards`, and `braking is back although no loop
  ran`. The second one is a run where STOP came before the first loop, which used to leave the next
  OpMode coasting.

Both were measured by putting each mistake back.
:::

:::{admonition} What has not been done here
:class: note
Steps 2 and 3 have not been performed on a robot for this page, and no test can stand in for them:
pushing a robot by hand is not something the simulator does. The arithmetic underneath is tested on
its own, in `CalibrationTest`, and step 1's messages are measured.
:::

## What you just did

You turned two constants into measurements. `Constants.java` was handed to you with 45.0 and 8.0 in
it, and those are somebody's robot, not yours. Nothing in L16 could tell they were wrong, because
code cannot check a number it has no other way of knowing.

The method is worth more than either number. Have the robot measure the same thing two ways, with
two instruments that do not talk to each other, and compare. That is how the wheel size, the
gearing, and the tuning numbers are all found, and it is what `task.autotune` does with the rest of
them.
